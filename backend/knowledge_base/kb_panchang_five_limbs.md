# Panchang Five Limbs

**Panchanga** literally means "five limbs". A Hindu almanac (panchang) reports five quantities for each day: **tithi** (lunar day), **vara** (weekday), **nakshatra** (the Moon's lunar mansion), **yoga** (a Sun-Moon sum) and **karana** (half-tithi). Together they describe the "quality of the moment" and are the raw inputs for muhurta (electional timing). All are derived from the sidereal longitudes of the Sun and Moon, except vara, which is simply the weekday. In traditional practice the day is named by whatever tithi is current at local **sunrise**.

These are traditional timing indicators, offered as guidance for reflection. They are not predictions, and no day is "dangerous"; an "unfavourable" quality only means tradition suggests choosing another time for new beginnings if there is a choice.

## Tithi: the lunar day

A **tithi** is the time it takes the Moon to gain 12 degrees on the Sun. With 360 degrees of elongation, a synodic month holds 30 tithis. Tithi number = floor((Moon longitude - Sun longitude, mod 360) / 12) + 1. Because the Moon's speed varies, tithis last roughly 19 to 26 hours and do not start or end at sunrise; a tithi can begin and end in the middle of a day, and occasionally a calendar date has two tithis' worth of sunrise (or none), which is why panchangs sometimes show a "skipped" or "repeated" tithi (Sewell and Dikshit describe the expunged and doubled tithi).

The two fortnights (**paksha**) are **Shukla** (bright, waxing, new moon to full moon) and **Krishna** (dark, waning, full moon to new moon). Each has tithis 1 to 15 with the same names. Shukla 15 is **Purnima** (full moon); Krishna 15 is **Amavasya** (new moon).

**Names:** 1 Pratipada, 2 Dwitiya, 3 Tritiya, 4 Chaturthi, 5 Panchami, 6 Shashthi, 7 Saptami, 8 Ashtami, 9 Navami, 10 Dashami, 11 Ekadashi, 12 Dwadashi, 13 Trayodashi, 14 Chaturdashi, 15 Purnima/Amavasya.

## Tithi deities (adhidevata)

A common list, with variants noted:

- 1 Agni
- 2 Brahma
- 3 Gauri (Parvati)
- 4 Ganesha (some lists: Yama)
- 5 Nagas / Sarpa
- 6 Kartikeya (Skanda)
- 7 Surya
- 8 Rudra / Shiva
- 9 Durga (some lists: the Mother-goddesses)
- 10 Dharmaraja / Yama
- 11 Vishvedevas (some lists: Vishnu or Rudra)
- 12 Vishnu
- 13 Kamadeva
- 14 Shiva (some lists: Kali)
- 15 Chandra (Purnima) or the Pitris (Amavasya)

These deities explain which observances attach to which tithi (Ganesha worship on Chaturthi, Vishnu fasting on Ekadashi, Shiva on Pradosha and Chaturdashi).

## Tithi classes (the five nature groups)

Muhurta tradition groups the 15 tithis into five repeating classes of three:

- **Nanda** (1, 6, 11): joy and celebration; favoured for festivals, music, decoration, starting artistic work.
- **Bhadra** (2, 7, 12): welfare and steadiness; favoured for ordinary auspicious tasks, farming, travel, medical treatment.
- **Jaya** (3, 8, 13): victory and effort; favoured for competition, negotiation, skilled work, learning crafts.
- **Rikta** (4, 9, 14): "empty"; traditionally avoided for new beginnings and auspicious ceremonies, regarded as better suited to dissolving, clearing, finishing or confrontational tasks.
- **Purna** (5, 10, 15): "full/complete"; favoured for auspicious and completing activities.

**Additional tithi notes (traditional):** Amavasya is generally avoided for ceremonies like marriage but is the principal day for ancestor rites. Purnima is broadly considered auspicious, especially for worship and charity. **Ashtami and Navami** are given mixed treatment (strong days for goddess worship, but frequently avoided for starting ceremonies). Class and weekday combinations (for example, a Nanda tithi on a particular weekday) are refined in some almanacs; these details differ by region and are not given here.

## Vara: weekday and its lord

The seven weekdays follow the order of the planets by the hora scheme (see the daily-timings file) and each has a lord:

- **Ravi/Aditya-vara (Sunday):** Sun. Authority, vitality, government matters, health regimen.
- **Soma/Chandra-vara (Monday):** Moon. Mind, home, nourishment, travel by water in some texts.
- **Mangala/Bhauma-vara (Tuesday):** Mars. Energy, property, courage, technical and mechanical work; traditionally less favoured for gentle, relationship-oriented beginnings.
- **Budha-vara (Wednesday):** Mercury. Learning, trade, writing, contracts, communication.
- **Guru/Brihaspati-vara (Thursday):** Jupiter. Teaching, ceremony, law, finance advice, spiritual practice.
- **Shukra-vara (Friday):** Venus. Relationships, art, luxury, vehicles, comforts.
- **Shani-vara (Saturday):** Saturn. Discipline, long-term structures, service, ironwork; traditionally less favoured for light-hearted beginnings.

Traditionally Moon, Mercury, Jupiter and Venus days are called "mild" (saumya) and Sun, Mars and Saturn days "sharp/fierce" (krura); many muhurta tables prefer mild days for auspicious ceremonies. The Hindu civil day begins at sunrise, so a vara runs from one sunrise to the next, not from midnight.

## Nakshatra as day quality

Because the Moon completes a sidereal circuit in about 27.3 days, it is in one of the 27 nakshatras (each 13 deg 20 min) on a given day. For panchang purposes the **day's nakshatra** is the one the Moon occupies at the relevant moment (usually sunrise, with transitions listed). The classical groups used in muhurta:

- **Dhruva / Sthira (fixed):** Rohini, Uttara Phalguni, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada. Suited to lasting things: foundations, planting, coronations, settling.
- **Chara / Chala (movable):** Punarvasu, Swati, Shravana, Dhanishta, Shatabhisha. Suited to travel, vehicles, change of place.
- **Ugra / Krura (fierce):** Bharani, Magha, Purva Phalguni, Purva Ashadha, Purva Bhadrapada. Traditionally better for forceful, defensive or competitive work than for gentle ceremonies.
- **Mridu / Maitra (soft):** Mrigashira, Chitra, Anuradha, Revati. Suited to friendships, art, clothes, ornaments, romance.
- **Kshipra / Laghu (swift):** Ashwini, Pushya, Hasta (and Abhijit in some schemes). Suited to trade, medicine, learning, quick starts.
- **Tikshna / Daruna (sharp):** Ardra, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Mula. Traditionally avoided for new ceremonial beginnings; used for removal and surgical-type work.
- **Mishra / Sadharana (mixed):** Krittika, Vishakha. Mixed results.

**Tara Bala (strength of the day star for you):** count from your **janma nakshatra** (birth star) to the day's nakshatra, counting your own as 1. Divide the count by 9 and take the remainder (a remainder of 0 means 9). The nine **Taras** are: 1 Janma (birth), 2 Sampat (wealth), 3 Vipat (obstacle), 4 Kshema (well-being), 5 Pratyari (opposition), 6 Sadhaka (accomplishment), 7 Vadha/Naidhana (loss), 8 Mitra (friend), 9 Ati-Mitra (best friend). Tradition regards Sampat, Kshema, Sadhaka, Mitra and Ati-Mitra as supportive, and Vipat, Pratyari and Vadha as the ones to avoid for important beginnings. Janma is usually treated as mixed (neutral to be cautious); some sources count the 10th and 19th stars as variants "Karma" and "Adhana" Janma-type stars. Treat as a refinement, not a rule that overrides everything else.

**Chandra Bala (strength of the Moon for you):** count the transiting Moon's sign from your natal Moon sign. The Moon in the 1st, 3rd, 6th, 7th, 10th or 11th from your natal Moon is the classical favourable set, with the 4th, 8th and 12th the commonly avoided set. Some almanacs treat 2nd, 5th and 9th as acceptable especially in the waxing fortnight [unverified]. See `kb_panchang_gochara_transit_rules.md` for the fuller Moon gochara scheme.

## Yoga: the 27 nitya yogas

Add the sidereal longitudes of the Sun and Moon (mod 360) and divide into 27 equal segments of 13 deg 20 min. The segment the sum falls in is the **nitya yoga**. A yoga lasts roughly 20 to 26 hours. (Do not confuse with planetary combinations, which are also called "yogas".)

In order: 1 **Vishkambha**, 2 **Priti**, 3 **Ayushman**, 4 **Saubhagya**, 5 **Shobhana**, 6 **Atiganda**, 7 **Sukarma**, 8 **Dhriti**, 9 **Shula**, 10 **Ganda**, 11 **Vriddhi**, 12 **Dhruva**, 13 **Vyaghata**, 14 **Harshana**, 15 **Vajra**, 16 **Siddhi**, 17 **Vyatipata**, 18 **Variyana**, 19 **Parigha**, 20 **Shiva**, 21 **Siddha**, 22 **Sadhya**, 23 **Shubha**, 24 **Shukla**, 25 **Brahma**, 26 **Indra** (Aindra), 27 **Vaidhriti**.

Traditional keyword meanings: Priti (affection), Ayushman (long life/vitality), Saubhagya (good fortune), Shobhana (beauty/splendour), Sukarma (good deeds), Dhriti (steadiness), Vriddhi (growth), Dhruva (fixed/stable), Harshana (joy), Siddhi (accomplishment), Variyana (comfort), Shiva (auspicious), Siddha (success), Sadhya (achievable), Shubha (auspicious), Shukla (bright/pure), Brahma (sacred/learning), Indra (power).

**Traditionally cautionary yogas (usually listed as nine):** Vishkambha, Atiganda, Shula, Ganda, Vyaghata, Vajra, Vyatipata, Parigha and Vaidhriti. Vyatipata and Vaidhriti are the two most strongly avoided for new ceremonies ("Mahapata"). Many almanacs also give a shorter caution for only parts of some of these yogas (for example, the first few ghatikas of Parigha or Vyaghata), and these sub-rules vary by region.

**Nitya yoga is a different thing from the Vimshottari or Jaimini yogas.** In a panchang context, "yoga" means this Sun-plus-Moon division.

## Karana: half a tithi

A **karana** is half a tithi: the time for the Moon to gain 6 degrees on the Sun. There are 60 karanas in a lunar month but only 11 names, in two groups. This structure is described in Sewell and Dikshit (art. 40).

**Seven repeating (chara) karanas:** Bava, Balava, Kaulava, Taitila, Gara, Vanija, **Vishti (Bhadra)**. They run in that order eight times (56 half-tithis), starting at the second half of Shukla Pratipada and ending at the first half of Krishna Chaturdashi.

**Four fixed (sthira) karanas:** **Shakuni** (second half of Krishna 14), **Chatushpada** (first half of Amavasya), **Naga** (second half of Amavasya), and **Kimstughna** (first half of Shukla Pratipada).

**Consequences of the arithmetic:** Vishti recurs eight times a month, always in the same half-tithis: second half of Shukla 4, first half of Shukla 8, second half of Shukla 11, first half of Shukla 15 (Purnima); second half of Krishna 3, first half of Krishna 7, second half of Krishna 10, first half of Krishna 14. (This follows directly from counting half-tithis from 1 and taking positions 8, 15, 22, 29, 36, 43, 50, 57.)

**Vishti / Bhadra:** The most commonly avoided karana. Bhadra is personified as a fierce figure (a daughter of the Sun and sister of Saturn in popular tradition). Almanacs list it as "traditionally avoided for starting auspicious or ceremonial work", with the common exceptions that it suits confrontational or protective work. Many panchangs refine this further with the Moon's sign: when the Moon is in particular signs, Bhadra is said to "reside" in the heavens, the earth or the underworld, and only the "earth-dwelling" type is held to carry the avoidance fully, while the other positions are considered mild; the sign groupings given by sources differ, so they are not reproduced here [unverified]. Some almanacs also pick out the "Bhadra-mukha" or "Bhadra-puccha" parts of the Vishti span as more or less avoided. The remaining karanas are generally regarded as workable; Shakuni, Chatushpada, Naga and Kimstughna are usually treated as best suited to specific ritual or protective tasks rather than new ventures.

## How the five limbs are used together

A typical panchang entry for a day reads: "Shukla Panchami, Guruvara, Pushya nakshatra, Siddha yoga, Balava karana." Traditional electional practice uses these in layers: avoid the clearly cautionary elements (Rikta tithi, Vishti karana, Vyatipata/Vaidhriti yoga, sharp nakshatras), favour supportive weekday-nakshatra-tithi combinations, then personalise using Tara Bala and Chandra Bala from the person's birth star and Moon sign. See `kb_panchang_muhurta_basics.md` for the layering and `kb_panchang_daily_timings.md` for intra-day windows.
