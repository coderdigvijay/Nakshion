# Compatibility by Relationship Type

## Marriage is the original use case

The Ashtakoota system was designed for **vivaha** (arranged marriage) in a Hindu cultural context. Its structure shows this:

- The tables are written for a **bride and a groom**, with the roles in a fixed order.
- **Yoni** reads instinctive and physical intimacy.
- **Nadi** and the Nadi/Bhakoot doshas are explained by traditional authors in terms of health and children.
- **Varna** and **Vashya** speak of influence, hierarchy and who adapts, which is a household-structure concept.
- Related systems (South Indian Porutham, Rajju, Mahendra and Stree Deergha) address longevity and the wife's wellbeing.

The Saravali documentation uses a neutral phrase, "partnership (or marriage) matching", but every factor it lists is a marriage factor. This is why the product should offer Ashtakoota only for romantic and marriage compatibility.

## What transfers to other relationships

Some Moon-based ideas apply to any pair of people, because the Moon describes the mind and emotional reflexes in all relationships:

- **Graha Maitri** (friendship between Moon-sign lords) is explicitly a measure of **mental rapport and friendship**. It is the koota most easily reused for friends, colleagues or siblings.
- **Gana** (temperament style) applies to any shared daily life such as flatmates or co-workers, though the scoring is built for marriage.
- **Tara (Tarabala)** is a general nakshatra-distance system used for muhurta and for any person-to-person link: the relationship of the other person's birth star to yours (Sampat, Kshema, Sadhana, Mitra and Parama Mitra are taken as supportive; Vipat, Pratyak and Naidhana as challenging). Secondary sources apply it to friends, family and colleagues [source: blog-level].
- **Moon sign relationships** between the two charts: same sign, 5/9 and 3/11 are generally read as easy; 6/8 and 2/12 as requiring more effort.

## Friendship and social ties

Vedic tradition frames friendship through the **3rd house** (companions, peers, siblings), the **11th house** (friends, networks, gains) and the **5th house** (affection, shared creativity). Compatibility is assessed by:

- Looking at the Moon, Mercury and the 3rd/11th lords in each chart and how they relate across the two.
- Comparing **Ascendant and Moon signs** for easy relationships (trine, same sign, 3/11).
- The **planetary friendship** (naisargika maitri) between the main planets of the two people.

Because no standard scoring table exists, anything presented as a "friendship score" is a modern design and should be described that way.

## Business partnership

Modern pages treat the **7th house** as the house of all committed partnerships including business, with the **10th** (career), **2nd/11th** (money and gains) and the **Ascendant** read together. Commonly recommended checks, all from secondary sources:

- The 7th house and its lord in each chart, and their relationship across the two charts.
- The 10th and 11th houses, for how the two people handle work and gains.
- Mercury (trade, contracts) and Jupiter (counsel and trust), by dignity and affliction.
- The dashas of both partners so the venture is not launched in a weak period for either [modern practice].
- Muhurta (an auspicious start time) for the venture itself, a separate and classical branch.

Sources agree on the general approach but give no unified method [unverified as a classical rule].

## Parent, child, siblings and teachers

Traditional house meanings carry the relationship: 4th (mother), 9th (father, teacher), 5th (children), 3rd (younger siblings), 11th (elder siblings). Compatibility with a family member is usually discussed using these houses and the relevant significators (Moon for mother, Sun for father, Jupiter for teachers and children), rather than any koota score. Tarabala can be used between two family members' birth stars.

## Cross-chart (synastry-style) tools in Vedic practice

Besides Ashtakoota, de Looff and other practitioners describe methods that suit any relationship type:

- **Cross-chart house placements:** where one person's planets fall in the other's houses (the Sudarshan Chakra overlay).
- **Tajika aspects between the charts:** inter-chart aspects with reduced orbs (de Looff suggests half the Tajika orbs, about 5 degrees at most, to avoid overload).
- **Ascendant and Moon relationships** and the quality of each person's Moon.

These are the Vedic equivalents of the Western synastry tools used elsewhere in this knowledge base, and they are generic enough for non-romantic pairs.

## Limits of Ashtakoota for non-romantic use

Reasons not to reuse the 36-point total for friendship or business:

1. **Yoni, Nadi, Bhakoot and Vashya** encode marriage and household assumptions; their zero scores have no standard meaning for a friendship.
2. **Gendered orientation.** The tables are asymmetric between bride and groom. Using them for two friends requires an arbitrary role assignment.
3. **The thresholds** (18, 25, 33) were set for marriage decisions in a specific tradition and have not been defined for other contexts.
4. **Single-factor focus.** The Moon alone is compared, so the results ignore the 3rd, 7th, 10th and 11th houses that matter for friendship and work.
5. **Same-sex and non-traditional couples.** The classical texts were written for a bride and groom. If a product applies Ashtakoota to other couples, it should say that the role assignment is a modern adaptation (for example, computing both orders), not a classical rule.

## Presenting non-romantic results

For friendship, family and business, a faithful summary would describe: Moon-sign relationship, Graha Maitri between Moon-sign lords, Tarabala between the birth stars, and cross-chart contacts between key planets, in plain language and without a "36-point score". These are tendencies, and conversation and shared goals play a larger role than any chart pattern.
