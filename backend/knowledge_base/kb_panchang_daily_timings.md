# Daily Timing Windows

Besides the five limbs, a panchang lists short windows within each day that tradition treats as more or less suitable for starting things. All of them are computed from **local sunrise and sunset**, so they shift with the season and the place. They are guidance only: the framing throughout is "traditionally avoided for new beginnings", not "dangerous".

## The basic arithmetic

Let **sunrise = SR** and **sunset = SS**, in local time.

- **Day length (dinamana)** = SS - SR.
- **Night length (ratrimana)** = next sunrise - SS.
- **Eighth-part length** = day length / 8. For a day of 12 h 00 min this is 90 minutes; for 13 h 12 min it is 99 minutes.
- Parts are counted 1 to 8 from sunrise.

A day running 06:00 to 18:00 gives parts of 06:00-07:30, 07:30-09:00, 09:00-10:30, 10:30-12:00, 12:00-13:30, 13:30-15:00, 15:00-16:30, 16:30-18:00. A hand-check example from a published panchang: day 06:53 to 18:59 gives 91-minute parts; Monday's Rahu Kaal (2nd part) is then 08:24 to 09:55, Yamaganda (4th) 11:25 to 12:56 and Gulika (6th) 14:27 to 15:58.

## Rahu Kaal (Rahu Kalam)

Rahu Kaal is the eighth-part of the daylight assigned to Rahu on each weekday. The part number by weekday:

| Weekday | Rahu Kaal part |
|---|---|
| Sunday | 8th |
| Monday | 2nd |
| Tuesday | 7th |
| Wednesday | 5th |
| Thursday | 6th |
| Friday | 4th |
| Saturday | 3rd |

A mnemonic often taught is the order Mon-Sat-Fri-Wed-Thu-Sun-Tue (parts 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8): Monday takes part 2, Saturday part 3, Friday part 4, Wednesday part 5, Thursday part 6, Tuesday part 7, Sunday part 8. Formula: start = SR + (n - 1) x day length / 8, end = start + day length / 8, where n is the part number from the table.

**Traditional framing:** many families in South India and elsewhere avoid starting travel, signing, launching, or beginning a ritual during Rahu Kaal, while ongoing routine work continues normally. It is a folk and almanac practice with regional strength rather than a core feature of the early classical muhurta texts [medium confidence]. Rahu Kaal on each day is simply a different slot, so there is always an alternative time available.

## Yamaganda (Yamagandam)

Another eighth-part of the day, traditionally regarded in almanacs as unfavourable for beginnings (the name is linked to Yama). Part numbers: Sunday 5th, Monday 4th, Tuesday 3rd, Wednesday 2nd, Thursday 1st, Friday 7th, Saturday 6th. Treated like Rahu Kaal: traditionally avoided for new beginnings, especially auspicious ceremonies.

## Gulika Kaal (Gulika Kalam)

**Gulika** is an upagraha (shadow-point) associated with Saturn's son. Its eighth-part runs through the weekdays in reverse order starting at Saturday: Saturday 1st, Friday 2nd, Thursday 3rd, Wednesday 4th, Tuesday 5th, Monday 6th, Sunday 7th. Almanacs generally list Gulika Kaal as a period to avoid for new beginnings, though some sources describe it as comparatively mild (and even supportive of things one hopes to keep stable); summaries vary, so present it as "traditionally avoided by many" and defer to the user's own tradition.

**Combined view:** a quick check of the three windows on any day tells you the stretches the user's tradition marks as "avoid for beginnings"; the remaining stretches are open. Weekday part tables above are consistent across the modern sources consulted.

## Abhijit Muhurta

The **muhurta** is a unit equal to one-thirtieth of a full day-night (48 minutes of 24 h). Traditional day division by sunrise-to-sunset treats the day as 15 muhurtas of (day length / 15) each. **Abhijit** is the 8th of the 15 daytime muhurtas, centred on local solar noon (the midpoint of SR and SS):

Abhijit start = SR + 7/15 x day length; end = SR + 8/15 x day length. On a 12-hour day this is 11:36 to 12:24 (48 minutes) if sunrise is 06:00. In general it equals the midpoint plus or minus (day length / 30).

It is traditionally regarded as a "victorious" window that largely overrides minor defects, so it is the one slot used for urgent beginnings when no better time exists. **Disagreement:** several almanacs state that Abhijit is not recommended on Wednesday [medium confidence]; some also hold that Abhijit is weakened when afflicted by certain yogas or Dur Muhurta overlap. In practice, almanac apps show Abhijit every day except where they apply this exclusion.

## Brahma Muhurta

The window before dawn traditionally valued for study, meditation and prayer. A muhurta being 48 minutes (day-night is 30 muhurtas), the **14th muhurta of the night** (counting from sunset) falls roughly 96 to 48 minutes before sunrise: start = SR - 96 min, end = SR - 48 min. Some sources instead define it as the period from 1 h 36 min to 48 min before sunrise, which is the same; others (using night length / 15) get slightly different numbers when the night is longer or shorter than 12 hours. Some modern articles loosely quote "4:00 to 5:30 a.m." without reference to sunrise; the sunrise-relative formula is the better form.

## Hora: planetary hours

A **hora** is a one-hour-ish division of the day, each ruled by a planet. There are 24 horas per sunrise-to-sunrise day: 12 daytime horas of (SS - SR)/12 and 12 night horas of (next SR - SS)/12. They are unequal across seasons ("seasonal hours"), unlike clock hours.

**Order of rulers:** Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, then repeating. This is the "Chaldean" order: the planets sorted by apparent orbital period (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon) and then read in steps of three places from the slowest. **The first hora of the day is ruled by the day's lord**, so Sunday starts with Sun, Monday with Moon, Tuesday with Mars, Wednesday with Mercury, Thursday with Jupiter, Friday with Venus, Saturday with Saturn. The first hora of the next day then lands three places along the cycle, which is why the weekday order is Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn.

**Example, Sunday:** Hora 1 Sun, 2 Venus, 3 Mercury, 4 Moon, 5 Saturn, 6 Jupiter, 7 Mars, 8 Sun, 9 Venus, 10 Mercury, 11 Moon, 12 Saturn; night horas 13 Jupiter, 14 Mars, 15 Sun, 16 Venus, 17 Mercury, 18 Moon, 19 Saturn, 20 Jupiter, 21 Mars, 22 Sun, 23 Venus, 24 Mercury. The next sunrise opens with the Moon, as expected for Monday.

**Typical uses (traditional):** Sun hora for authority and government dealings; Moon for public, care and travel matters; Mars for effort, repairs, competitions; Mercury for trade, writing, study; Jupiter for teaching, ceremony, advice; Venus for relationships, art, purchases of comforts; Saturn for discipline and long-term structure (traditionally less favoured for light beginnings). Treat as a minor refinement beneath tithi, nakshatra and the avoid-windows.

## Other short windows seen in panchangs (brief)

- **Dur Muhurta** and **Varjyam**: short windows marked as "avoid" that depend on weekday and nakshatra; formulas vary by almanac.
- **Choghadiya:** a folk eighth-part system (Udveg, Chal, Labh, Amrit, Kaal, Shubh, Rog) popular in western India for travel; it also divides the day into 8 parts and rotates by weekday, but is not a classical muhurta technique [medium confidence].
- **Pradosha:** the twilight about 1.5 hours around sunset on Trayodashi, observed for Shiva worship.

## Implementation note for software

Given latitude/longitude, date and time zone, compute sunrise and sunset (for the astronomical definition, the upper limb of the Sun at the horizon, with refraction), apply the formulas above, and expose the sunrise convention used. Polar and very high-latitude dates need special handling (no sunrise). Different almanacs adopt different sunrise definitions (disc centre vs upper limb), changing windows by a minute or two, which is normal.
