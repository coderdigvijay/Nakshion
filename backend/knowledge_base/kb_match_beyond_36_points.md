# Beyond the 36 Points

## Why the 36 points are only a starting layer

Ashtakoota reads one factor from each person, the Moon, and compares it with the other Moon. The Saravali documentation itself lists three cautions: the method should not be the only source of judgement; each person's own chart should be examined first; and the mutual positions of the **Moon, Ascendant and Venus** between the two charts deserve attention. A pair can score 30 out of 36 and still have difficult dashas, or score 20 and have strong 7th-house indications.

Practitioners such as the BAVA article's author note that married couples with low scores can be content and those with high scores can struggle, and they place the astrologer's counselling above the arithmetic. The sections below are the other layers usually consulted.

## The seventh house and its lord

The **7th house** (Kalatra bhava, house of the partner) is the first place any Parashari astrologer looks. Standard points, many already found in BPHS:

- The condition of the **7th lord**: strength, dignity, and the house it occupies. BPHS treats a strong or exalted 7th lord, supported by a strong Lagna lord and a benefic, as a favourable indication, and a 7th lord that is weak or in the 6th, 8th or 12th as a sign of strain in the partnership. The text uses categorical language for results; modern practice reads these as tendencies.
- **Planets in the 7th**: benefics are read as supporting harmony; malefics (Saturn, Mars, Rahu, Ketu) are read as adding intensity, delay or friction depending on dignity and aspects.
- The 7th is also checked **from the Moon and from Venus** in many traditions, and the 2nd (family) and 8th (the depth and continuity of the bond) are looked at alongside it.

The two charts are then compared: for example whether one partner's 7th lord is well placed in the other's chart, and whether the two Lagnas or Moons stand in supportive signs (5/9 and same sign are usually taken as good in general practice, although Ashtakoota's Bhakoot koota scores 5/9 as 0 and some authors dispute that; 3/11 is workable).

## Venus and Jupiter as relationship significators

**Venus** is the natural significator (karaka) of love, marriage and comfort, and is assessed in every marriage reading in a man's or woman's chart. **Jupiter** is the traditional karaka of the husband in a woman's chart and is considered a protective planet generally. Practitioners check:

- Sign dignity and house of Venus (and Jupiter), whether combust, retrograde or conjoined with malefics.
- The **Mars-Venus** relationship, which Jayasree Saranathan and others read as a measure of passion and its management.
- Whether Jupiter aspects the 7th house or the 7th lord. A Jupiter aspect is described as protective in the sources consulted.

Specific outcomes quoted in BPHS (for example about multiple spouses or loss of a spouse) are phrased in absolute terms. This knowledge base does not repeat those statements as predictions.

## Navamsa (D9)

The **Navamsa** chart divides each sign into nine parts of 3 degrees 20 minutes and is regarded as the "chart of the spouse" and of the mature fruits of relationships. Jagannath Hora's summary presents the usual approach:

1. Establish what the main (D1) chart promises.
2. Look at the **7th house and 7th lord in D9**, the position of Venus in D9 and the D9 Lagna.
3. Note **Vargottama** planets (same sign in D1 and D9), which are read as reinforced.
4. Compare the D9 7th houses and lords of the two partners.

Navamsa is used as a **refinement**, not as an override. Some traditions also cite Navamsa signs when evaluating a Nadi-dosha cancellation, which is a classical link between the Moon-only 36-point system and the finer divisional chart.

## Moon, mind and emotional fit

Because the Moon governs mind and emotional response, Moon-based comparisons carry the most weight in the 36-point system. Beyond Ashtakoota practitioners look at:

- The **Moon sign relationship** between the two charts (same sign, 5/9 and 3/11 are generally easier; 6/8 and 2/12 are the bhakoot patterns).
- **Moon-Venus links** between the charts (for example one person's Moon conjunct or in trine to the other's Venus), commonly given weight in practical readings.
- The **strength and affliction of each Moon** in its own chart: a stressed Moon changes how the koota results are read.
- **Graha Maitri** between the sign lords, as a measure of mental friendship.

Roeland de Looff recommends comparing the Ascendants, Moons, Sun, Mercury, Venus and Mars of the two charts, and treating the Moon and Venus as especially significant.

## Dasha and transit compatibility

Timing is part of compatibility. Practitioners look at:

- The **Vimshottari mahadasha and antardasha** each partner is in. Periods of the 7th lord, Venus and Jupiter, and of planets connected to the 7th, are watched for relationship events.
- Whether the two partners' dasha lords are friendly or hostile to each other, or whether both are entering demanding periods (such as a Saturn or Rahu period) at the same time. This kind of **dasha comparison** is common modern practice, and a consistent classical rule for it could not be verified [unverified].
- **Transits** over the 7th house, the Moon and Venus; commonly cited: Jupiter's influence on the 7th or 7th lord is read as supportive, and Saturn's pressure as testing.
- For marriage timing in particular, BPHS gives long lists of age-specific combinations for the 7th lord, Venus and the Navamsa; modern practitioners use these mainly as pointers to which dasha periods to examine.

## Upapada Lagna (Jaimini)

In the Jaimini system the **Upapada Lagna (UL)** is the arudha (image) of the 12th house and is the principal marker of marriage and the spouse. Commonly taught points:

- The **UL sign and its lord** describe the quality and public face of the relationship; a well-placed UL lord is read as favourable.
- The **2nd house from UL** shows the sustenance and longevity of the marriage. Jaimini's text, as quoted by secondary sources, says that if the 2nd from UL is conjoined by its own lord or the lord is in its own or exalted sign, any loss of a partner comes only late in life; malefic influences on it are read as strain on duration.
- The **7th and 8th from UL** are used in Chara-dasha timing and for the question of repeated marriages. [Single set of secondary sources.]
- Jaimini method uses Chara dasha and arudha-based aspects that differ from the Parashari scheme; the existing Jaimini note in this knowledge base covers the mechanics.

## Age, maturity and practical life factors

No classical text is used here to claim an "ideal" age for marriage or an age after which Manglik dosha ends. The sources checked specifically say that the "after age 28" rule for Mars is not found in classical texts (see the doshas note). A counselling approach that many practitioners describe takes account of:

- The **ages and life stages** of both people, and whether either is in a Saturn-return or a major dasha change.
- The balance between strong and weak indicators across both charts rather than any single score.
- Shared values, communication habits and family circumstances, which no chart contains.

## A practical order of reading

1. Each natal chart on its own: 7th house, 7th lord, Venus, Jupiter, Mars, Moon.
2. Navamsa and the Upapada Lagna of each person.
3. Ashtakoota and the doshas, with their cancellations.
4. Cross-chart contacts between the Moons, Ascendants and Venuses.
5. Dasha periods now and ahead.
6. A balanced summary: strengths, areas to discuss, and a reminder that the chart describes tendencies and that free will and conversation matter.
