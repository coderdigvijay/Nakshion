# Alternative Dasha Systems

## Important: Nakshion uses Vimshottari

Nakshion computes and explains **Vimshottari** dasha only. The three systems below are described for completeness and for answering general questions, so the assistant can acknowledge that other systems exist without presenting them as the app's own output. Never present another system's periods as if they were computed for the user, unless the app actually computes them.

## Why there are many systems

The classical text *Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra* (BPHS) is said to list dozens of dasha systems (sources speak of about 42), of which Vimshottari is the "general" one and most others are conditional: they apply only when a particular chart condition is met. Varahamihira's *Brihat Jataka* describes yet another approach based on planetary strength and house position. Modern practitioners mostly use Vimshottari, sometimes complemented with Yogini, Ashtottari or Jaimini's Chara dasha for cross-checking. There is no scientific consensus on which is best; many astrologers choose by tradition, region or personal experience.

## Yogini dasha (36 years)

**What it is.** A nakshatra-based cycle of 36 years governed by eight "Yoginis" (goddess-like powers), each associated with a planet. Order and years:

| Yogini | Planet | Years |
|---|---|---|
| Mangala | Moon | 1 |
| Pingala | Sun | 2 |
| Dhanya | Jupiter | 3 |
| Bhramari | Mars | 4 |
| Bhadrika | Mercury | 5 |
| Ulka | Saturn | 6 |
| Siddha | Venus | 7 |
| Sankata | Rahu | 8 |

Total: 36 years (1+2+...+8), repeating about three times to cover a lifetime.

**Calculation.** Commonly stated: take the Moon's nakshatra number (Ashwini = 1), add 3, divide by 8; the remainder (1 to 8, with 0 meaning 8) picks the starting Yogini in the order above. For example, Ardra (6) gives 9 mod 8 = 1 = Mangala. The balance of that first period is computed like Vimshottari, from the fraction of the nakshatra remaining. [verify the "+3" rule against a primary text before implementing]. Antardashas are proportional to the Yoginis' years.

**When it applies.** It is a general-purpose short-cycle system and is often used as a second opinion, especially for finer timing, because its periods are short. Some authors reserve it for particular conditions; the traditions differ.

**Character.** Mangala, Dhanya, Bhadrika and Siddha are traditionally seen as more favourable; Pingala, Ulka and Sankata as more demanding; Bhramari as active and mobile. Treat these as general flavours, not predictions.

## Ashtottari dasha (108 years)

**What it is.** A nakshatra-based cycle of 108 years ("ashtottari" = 108) with eight lords, excluding Ketu. A commonly cited year set is: Sun 6, Moon 15, Mars 8, Mercury 17, Saturn 10, Jupiter 19, Rahu 12, Venus 21 (total 108). This set appears in both the public-domain Vijnananda translation note on Brihat Jataka and in modern summaries. Another website lists a different year set that conflicts with this and with the total, so treat that as an error or variant [unverified].

**Applicability.** BPHS is usually said to prescribe it only in certain charts. The commonly cited conditions are Rahu located in a kendra or trikona (from the Lagna, or from the Lagna lord, depending on the source), and in some formulations birth in daytime in Krishna paksha or at night in Shukla paksha. Authorities describe the conditions differently, and they are widely considered controversial [school-dependent]. If the conditions are not met, Vimshottari is used instead.

**Nakshatra mapping.** Some sources start from Ardra (Sun) and count 28 nakshatras including Abhijit; the Vijnananda note instead groups nakshatras starting from Krittika (Sun: Krittika, Rohini, Mrigashira), which is a different arrangement. Because sources conflict, no mapping is given here [unverified].

**Character.** Practitioners sometimes use it as a check on Vimshottari when Vimshottari seems not to fit, particularly for Rahu-prominent charts.

## Chara dasha (Jaimini)

**What it is.** A **sign-based** (rashi) dasha from Jaimini's school: each sign, not each planet, rules a period, starting from the Lagna. Periods vary from 1 to 12 years depending on the sign. It is used with Jaimini techniques (Chara karakas, Arudha padas, Karakamsa), not with nakshatras, and therefore does not depend on the Moon's nakshatra position.

**Duration of a sign period.** Count from the sign to the sign occupied by its lord, then subtract one. If the lord is in its own sign, the sign gets 12 years (in the source consulted). Some traditions add one year if the lord is exalted and subtract one if debilitated. Duration is therefore chart-specific. Example: Aries with Mars in Leo: count Aries 1, Taurus 2, Gemini 3, Cancer 4, Leo 5; minus 1 = 4 years.

**Direction.** The direction of counting to the lord, and the direction of the sequence of dasha signs, depends on whether the sign is "odd-footed" or "even-footed" in one tradition, and on a different grouping in others (the schools of K.N. Rao and Sanjay Rath, among others, differ on details). Sources consulted disagree in presentation, so rules are not reproduced here [unverified]. The treatment of dual-lord signs (Scorpio, Aquarius) with Rahu/Ketu as co-lords also differs.

**When it applies.** Jaimini practitioners treat Chara dasha as their primary dasha for events such as marriage, career, and relocations. Parashari practitioners may use it as a cross-check. It is not widely implemented in consumer software because of the method disputes above.

## Choosing and presenting alternatives

- State which system a statement refers to. Mixing systems produces contradictions.
- Prefer one primary system (Vimshottari in Nakshion) and treat others as supplementary.
- Where a user asks about a different system, explain it generally and note that Nakshion does not compute it.
- Avoid claiming one system is "the real one"; traditions differ.
