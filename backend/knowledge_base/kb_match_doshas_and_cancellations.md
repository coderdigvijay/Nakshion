# Matching Doshas and Their Cancellations

## What "dosha" means in matching

In horoscope matching, a **dosha** is a flagged configuration that traditional authors considered worth attention: a koota scoring zero, or a Mars placement in a sensitive house. The word is often translated as "defect" or "affliction", but a better working reading is **"area to look at more closely"**. Nearly every tradition pairs each dosha with a list of **parihara** (cancellations or mitigations), and practitioners disagree on how heavily to weigh the dosha at all.

Guidance for how this knowledge base should be used in conversation:

- Describe a dosha as a **tendency or theme** (for example "the Nadi koota is at zero, which traditionally points to similar constitutional patterns"), never as a prediction of illness, separation or death.
- Always give the cancellations that apply to the specific pair, and say that practitioners weigh them differently.
- Remind the reader that matching is advisory and that real compatibility also depends on communication, values and the full charts (see the companion note on factors beyond 36 points).

## Nadi Dosha

**Trigger.** Both Moons fall in nakshatras of the same nadi (Adi, Madhya or Antya). The Nadi koota then scores 0 of 8, which is the largest single loss in the 36-point system.

**Traditional theme.** Nadi is linked to the ayurvedic humours (Vata, Pitta, Kapha). The classical concern is the similarity of constitutions, which is connected in the texts to health and children. The concern is stated as a theme to discuss; it is not a diagnosis.

**Cancellations commonly listed** (secondary sources, most to least widely repeated):

1. **Same Moon sign, different nakshatra.**
2. **Same nakshatra, different pada** (quarter).
3. **Different Moon signs** while the nadi is the same: the often-quoted principle attributed in secondary sources to Muhurta Chintamani [unverified; sources disagree on exact wording].
4. **Different navamsa signs** for the two Moons, attributed to Keshava Daivajna's Vivaha Vrindavan [unverified; single secondary source].
5. A **benefic Jupiter or Venus influence on the 7th house** in the charts [single source, unverified].
6. Some texts subdivide each nadi and treat partners from different subgroups as only partly affected.

**How modern astrologers weigh it.** Most online and practising astrologers regard Nadi dosha as one of the more serious flags but also the one with the most numerous exceptions. A same-nadi pair with an exception such as 1, 2 or 3 above is commonly treated as functionally fine; without any exception, practitioners typically recommend looking at the 7th house and other factors rather than concluding anything from the 0.

## Bhakoot Dosha

**Trigger.** The two Moon signs are 2/12, 5/9 or 6/8 from each other. The Bhakoot koota scores 0 of 7.

**The three types and their themes:**

- **Shadashtak (6/8):** traditionally associated with friction, health worries and difference of temperament. It is regarded as the most serious of the three.
- **Dwirdwadash (2/12):** associated with money and household rhythm.
- **Nav-Pancham (5/9):** associated with children and differing life purposes. Several authors do not count 5/9 as a dosha at all, and many consider it a harmonious trine. When classical lists are cited, this is the entry that most often disagrees.

**Cancellations commonly listed:**

1. **Same Moon-sign lord** (for example Aries/Scorpio, Taurus/Libra, Capricorn/Aquarius).
2. **Friendly Moon-sign lords** (for example Sun/Moon in a Leo-Cancer 2/12 pair).
3. **Parivartana (exchange)** between the Moon-sign lords.
4. Same **nakshatra lord** for both Moons.
5. Strong **Graha Maitri** (5 of 5), strong Nadi (8 of 8) and a high overall total.
6. The distinction some authors draw between **Priti** (affectionate) and **Mrityu** (harmful) shadashtak: when the sign lords are friends the 6/8 axis is treated as benign, and a "Subha Dwirdwadash" when the groom's sign is the second of the 2/12 pair [single source].

One secondary source reports that, in a pooled count, about 8 percent of bhakoot doshas disappear under the same-lord rule alone and about 47 percent once friendly lords are allowed [unverified; source unnamed].

## Gana Dosha

**Trigger.** In the common (Saravali) matrix, a Rakshasa paired with a non-Rakshasa scores 0, except Rakshasa bride with Deva groom, which scores 1. A score of 0 or 1 is often labelled "Gana dosha". Other matrices give different values (see the Ashtakoota note).

**Traditional theme.** Gana describes temperament: how each person handles disagreement, pace and assertiveness. A Rakshasa Moon nakshatra is a label for a forceful, independent style, not a moral failing.

**Cancellations commonly listed:**

1. Same Moon-sign lord for both.
2. Friendly Moon-sign lords (strong Graha Maitri).
3. **Same Moon sign** (Eka Rashi).
4. Same nakshatra lord.
5. Birth stars more than 14 nakshatras apart [single source; Hora Sarvam; unverified].
6. Full points in Nadi and good Tara and Yoni, or a high overall score such as above 26 [modern rule of thumb, not classical].

## Manglik (Kuja / Mangal) Dosha: the rule

**What it is.** The configuration in which Mars occupies certain houses from a reference point. Mars is the planet of drive, heat and friction, and the concern is about how that energy shows up in a partnership.

**Houses.** Mars in the **1st, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th** is the core North Indian list. South Indian practice also includes the **2nd**, giving the "1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 12" list commonly seen on websites.

**Reference points.** Later tradition counts the placement from the **Lagna (ascendant), the Moon and Venus**, each examined separately.

**Primary text.** In the English BPHS edition consulted here, the verse states a severe outcome for the spouse's longevity (in absolute terms, which this knowledge base does not repeat as a prediction) if Mars is in the 12th, 4th, 7th or 8th from the Lagna without any benefic aspect or association, and the following verses say the same applies to a man and that the effect ceases when a man and woman who both have such a yoga marry. Another source quotes the same verse as including the Lagna itself. Chapter and verse numbers differ between editions (chapter 80 in this edition, 81 in others). The verse mentions Lagna only; the Moon and Venus reference points appear in later texts [secondary sources].

**Cautions about frequency.** With three reference points and six houses, a large share of charts will qualify. One practitioner writes that about 80 percent do; this is an author estimate and not a statistic. Wikipedia describes the belief as a social superstition. Both observations support treating it as a flag to discuss, not a barrier.

## Manglik Dosha: cancellations

**From the BPHS text itself:** benefic aspect or conjunction on Mars; and both partners having the yoga ("mutual cancellation").

**Commonly cited sign-based rules** (Jataka Chandrika's house-wise table, as quoted by Ashish Desai; the quoted list was re-checked against that page on 2026-10-02, but other authors list different signs [single source; lists vary]):

| Mars in house | Reduced when Mars is in |
|---|---|
| 1st | Aries, Leo, Aquarius |
| 2nd | Aries, Scorpio, Gemini, Virgo |
| 4th | Aries, Scorpio, Taurus, Libra |
| 7th | Cancer, Capricorn |
| 8th | Sagittarius, Pisces |
| 12th | Taurus, Libra |

**Other widely cited reductions:** Mars in its own sign or exalted; Jupiter's aspect on Mars or on the 7th house (valid only if Jupiter is a functional benefic for that Lagna); Mars conjunct Jupiter or Mercury; Mars as a yogakaraka for Cancer or Leo Lagna; a Mars that is combust. Some sources add Moon-Mars or Venus-Jupiter links. The **"Mars dosha fades after age 28"** idea appears on many sites; Desai notes that no classical text specifies it, so treat it as folk practice [single source].

**Weighing.** Most authors rank the reductions roughly as: structural (yogakaraka), strong (Jupiter aspect, own sign or exaltation), moderate (benefic conjunctions) and unreliable (age rule). Practitioners recommend assessing the strength of Mars itself and the 7th house, not counting houses alone.

**Rituals.** Some traditions recommend symbolic marriages (to a tree or vessel) as a "remedy". These are cultural practices and are not endorsed here; remedial astrology is covered in a separate file.

## How modern astrologers weigh doshas

Counselling-oriented authors (for example the BAVA article by a practising Vedic counsellor) argue that mechanical scoring is secondary: they note that marriages with low scores can be happy and high-scoring couples can struggle. A practical workflow often described:

1. Check which doshas appear, and which cancellations apply.
2. Look at the quality of the 7th house, 7th lord, Venus and Mars in each natal chart.
3. Check the dashas running now and in the next few years.
4. Present the results as strengths to build on and areas to discuss, with constructive framing.
