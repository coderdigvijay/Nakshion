# Gochara and Dasha Interplay

## The core idea: dasha sets the season, transit sets the moment

In Vedic timing, the **dasha** system indicates *which planetary themes* are active over months to years, while **gochara** (transits) shows *when within that period* the themes become visible. A common summary: the dasha names the season, the transit names the days. A transit usually signals nothing strong unless the running dasha lord, or a planet connected to it, supports the same theme.

Practical hierarchy for a reading: (1) natal promise (does the chart hold the theme at all?), (2) dasha and antardasha support, (3) transits of the slow planets, (4) fast planets for short windows. Present results as periods of emphasis, never as dates of certain events.

## Gochara from the Moon: baseline favourable houses

Classically, transit results are counted from the **natal Moon sign** (Chandra Lagna), with the Lagna used as a secondary reference. Favourable houses from the Moon, as commonly listed:

| Planet | Favourable houses from Moon |
|---|---|
| Saturn | 3, 6, 11 (cross-checked) |
| Jupiter | 2, 5, 7, 9, 11 (cross-checked) |
| Rahu / Ketu | 3, 6, 11 (cross-checked, case-by-case) |
| Sun | 3, 6, 10, 11 [unverified here] |
| Moon | 1, 3, 6, 7, 10, 11 [unverified here] |
| Mars | 3, 6, 11 [unverified here] |
| Mercury | 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11 [unverified here] |
| Venus | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 11, 12 [unverified here] |

Saturn is listed as difficult from the Moon in 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 12 (some also add 7, 9, 10), and Jupiter in all houses not listed above (1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12; one source gave only 3, 6, 8, 12). This is a baseline, which the other factors below refine.

## Vedha (obstruction)

In the classical scheme a favourable transit can be blocked when another planet occupies the paired obstructing house at the same time. For Saturn: 3 is obstructed by 12, 6 by 9, 11 by 5. For Jupiter: 2 by 12, 5 by 4, 7 by 3, 9 by 10, 11 by 8. Vedha is applied less by modern practitioners than Ashtakavarga, and some schools skip it for planets that are not natural enemies. Treat it as a secondary refinement.

## Double transit (Jupiter + Saturn)

The **double transit** principle, popular through the K.N. Rao tradition, says that significant events tied to a house tend to occur when **both Jupiter and Saturn** influence that house (or its lord) at the same time, either by occupation or by aspect. Jupiter is read as supplying opportunity, growth or permission; Saturn as supplying structure, commitment or completion.

**Aspects used (Parashari).** Jupiter aspects the 5th, 7th and 9th from itself (and the 1st by occupancy); Saturn aspects the 3rd, 7th and 10th from itself. These are counted in sign aspects.

**How it is used.**
1. Choose the house or planet for the theme (for example the 7th house and its lord for partnership; the 10th for career).
2. Check whether the running dasha/antardasha lords connect to those significators.
3. Identify the months when transiting Jupiter and Saturn both touch the target sign (or its lord's sign or the natal Moon).
4. Prefer windows where the dasha supports the theme; transit alone is not considered sufficient.

Reported use narrows a multi-year dasha down to a handful of months. It remains a heuristic. Because Jupiter spends about a year per sign and Saturn about 2.5, genuine double-transit windows are not rare, and over-interpretation is a risk; use it to describe *periods when a theme is more likely to be active*.

## Ashtakavarga bindu strength

**Ashtakavarga** gives each planet a score from 0 to 8 in every sign, based on benefic points (bindus) contributed by seven planets and the Lagna. Summing the seven planets' scores gives the **Sarvashtakavarga** (SAV) per sign.

- Across all seven planets and twelve signs the bindus total **337**, an average of about **28** per sign. Signs above 28 are read as having more capacity to support the area they represent; below 28, less.
- For an individual planet (**Bhinnashtakavarga**, BAV), a transiting planet crossing a sign where its own score is **4 or more** is often read as able to deliver its results; **5 or more** is stronger; 0-2 suggests little support or more strain. Thresholds are rules of thumb that practitioners vary.
- A transit that is favourable by the Moon-based table but has a low bindu count is read as muted; an unfavourable transit with high bindus is read as softer.
- **Kakshya** (sub-division of each sign into 3 degrees 45 minutes zones, each ruled by one contributor) refines timing within a sign. This is an advanced step.

A commonly stated three-way test: dasha supports the theme, Ashtakavarga score for the transited house is favourable, and no strong obstruction (vedha, affliction). Where all three line up, a theme is described as more likely to be visible; otherwise it is muted.

## Timing windows and planet speeds

| Planet | Approx. time per sign | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Moon | about 2.25 days | Day-level triggers, mood, daily rhythm |
| Sun | about 1 month | Monthly emphasis |
| Mercury/Venus | about 3-5 weeks | Short-term communication, relationships |
| Mars | about 1.5 months | Energy, action windows |
| Jupiter | about 1 year | Growth and opportunity windows |
| Rahu/Ketu | about 1.5 years | Shifts of focus, restlessness |
| Saturn | about 2.5 years | Long structural periods (see Sade Sati) |

Retrograde motion lengthens or interrupts a sign stay, which is why ingress dates should be computed from daily ephemeris data.

## Putting it together in an explanation

A grounded answer to a timing question follows this pattern:

1. **Natal promise:** state what the chart says about the theme (house, lord, dignity).
2. **Dasha:** name the Mahadasha and Antardasha and whether the lords relate to the theme.
3. **Transit:** note where Jupiter and Saturn are relative to the Moon and relevant houses, plus any Sade Sati or Dhaiya in effect.
4. **Strength:** mention Ashtakavarga support if computed.
5. **Language:** speak of "windows of emphasis" and practical preparations; avoid dates and certainties; never contradict computed chart positions.

## Cautions

- Gochara rules were designed as a supplement to natal and dasha analysis, not a stand-alone system.
- Ashtakavarga requires an exact Lagna; with unknown birth time, use Moon-based analysis only and say so.
- Different schools weight transits from the Lagna versus from the Moon differently; state which reference is used.
