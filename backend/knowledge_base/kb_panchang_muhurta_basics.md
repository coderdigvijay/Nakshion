# Muhurta Basics

**Muhurta** (also **muhurtha**, electional astrology) is the tradition of choosing a favourable time to begin something. It is built on the five limbs of the panchang (see `kb_panchang_five_limbs.md`), the daily windows (see `kb_panchang_daily_timings.md`), the planetary strength of the lagna and the Moon at the chosen moment, and the personal fit with the person's own birth star and Moon sign. The classic reference is the **Muhurta Chintamani** of Rama Daivajna (Daivagya), with the older Narada Samhita and Brihat Samhita supplying earlier layers.

**Framing for this knowledge base:** muhurta is a **traditional practice for choosing among options**. It suggests when a time may be "traditionally preferred" or "traditionally avoided for new beginnings". It does not predict outcomes, replaces no professional judgement, and cannot make a poor plan work or a good plan fail. When no choice is available (an urgent trip, a fixed hospital appointment), tradition itself says to go ahead, often with a short prayer or by using Abhijit.

## The layered method

A typical order of checks, from broad to fine:

1. **Month and solar period.** Is it a period the tradition avoids for the rite (Adhika masa, Chaturmas for certain ceremonies, Kharmas, "asta" of Jupiter or Venus for weddings)?
2. **Tithi.** Avoid Rikta (4, 9, 14), Amavasya and, for many rites, the 8th; prefer Nanda, Bhadra, Jaya, Purna classes suited to the task.
3. **Vara (weekday).** Match the rite to a weekday lord (for example a Thursday for learning or ceremony).
4. **Nakshatra.** Use the nakshatra groups (fixed, movable, soft, swift, and so on) and avoid the sharp group for ceremonies.
5. **Yoga and karana.** Avoid Vyatipata and Vaidhriti and other cautionary yogas, and avoid Vishti (Bhadra).
6. **Lagna (rising sign) and planets at the moment.** Prefer a lagna whose lord is strong, benefics in kendras (1, 4, 7, 10) and trikonas (5, 9), no malefic in the 8th, and a Moon that is waxing and not afflicted. Avoid eclipses and a debilitated or combust lagna lord.
7. **Personal checks.** Tara Bala from the person's birth star and Chandra Bala from the birth Moon sign; avoid Chandrashtama (Moon 8th from natal Moon).
8. **Daily windows.** Avoid Rahu Kaal, Yamaganda, Gulika where observed; consider Abhijit.

Strong combined days, called **Sarvartha Siddhi**, **Amrita Siddhi** and **Ravi Pushya**-type days, are weekday-nakshatra pairings that almanacs list as especially supportive; the precise pairings are given in the almanacs and vary slightly between them [not reproduced here].

## Common avoid-lists (traditional)

- **Tithi:** Amavasya and Rikta tithis for new beginnings; certain tithis for specific rites (for example Ashtami and Chaturdashi for some ceremonies).
- **Karana:** Vishti (Bhadra), especially for ceremonies.
- **Yoga:** Vyatipata, Vaidhriti (strongest), and the other seven cautionary yogas.
- **Nakshatra:** Sharp group (Ardra, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Mula) for ceremonies; Gandanta junctions (the last part of Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Revati and the first part of Ashwini, Magha, Mula) are traditionally avoided for beginnings and for birth-related rites.
- **Moon:** Chandrashtama; a Moon that is very dim (close to the new moon) or conjunct harsh planets.
- **Days:** Eclipse days and the period around them (see below); the **Panchaka** days.
- **Daily windows:** Rahu Kaal, Yamaganda, Gulika (regional).

## Panchaka (Panchak)

**Panchaka** is the period of roughly five days when the Moon passes through the last five nakshatras: the second half of **Dhanishta**, **Shatabhisha**, **Purva Bhadrapada**, **Uttara Bhadrapada** and **Revati** (that is, Aquarius and Pisces in the sidereal zodiac). Traditional avoidance lists include starting a new roof or construction, collecting firewood or fuel, travelling in the south direction, preparing a cot or bed, and cremation without a prescribed pacifying rite. Some sources differentiate the Panchaka by weekday it begins on (Raja, Agni, Chora, Roga, Mrityu Panchaka, with mixed reputations); these names appear in popular almanacs and their treatment varies, so mention them only if the user's tradition does. As always, daily life and routine work go on normally; the idea is only to avoid beginning specific rites. [Source agreement: moderate; names of the weekday-Panchaka types are secondary-source only.]

## Eclipses (Grahana) in muhurta

Eclipse periods are traditionally avoided for starting auspicious work or for eating, and are used for prayer, mantra repetition, bathing and charity. See `kb_panchang_eclipses_and_special_days.md` for details. Muhurta lists usually also avoid a window of several days around an eclipse for weddings [medium-low confidence; length varies].

## Chaturmas

**Chaturmas** ("four months") is the period from the Ashadha Shukla Ekadashi (Devshayani/Shayani Ekadashi), when Vishnu is said to rest, to the Kartika Shukla Ekadashi (Prabodhini/Devutthani Ekadashi), when he wakes, spanning the rainy season and covering roughly July to November. Observances include simpler diet, fasting and study. In many traditions **marriage, upanayana (sacred thread), griha pravesh and other major ceremonies are traditionally not held** in this period, and the beginning of the "wedding season" is marked at the waking Ekadashi. Daily work, routine worship, education and ordinary travel go on. When an Adhika month falls in the Ashadha-Shravana window, the dates shift by about a month.

## Adhika masa (the extra month)

Lunar months average 29.53 days and solar months about 30.44, so a lunar year is about 11 days short. To realign, a lunar month **with no solar ingress (sankranti)** is inserted about every 32.5 months; it is the **Adhika masa**, also called Mala masa or Purushottama masa. It takes the name of the following regular month. Dharmashastra tradition generally keeps routine sacred acts and charity going but defers **new ceremonial events (weddings, griha pravesh, mundan, first-feeding)** to a regular month. On rare occasions a month with two ingresses is dropped (**Kshaya masa**), which is a theoretical and historically rare event.

Related solar-period cautions that appear in muhurta tables: **Kharmas/Malmas** (Sun in Dhanu and in Meena, roughly mid-December to mid-January and mid-March to mid-April), when North Indian tradition defers weddings; and the **asta (combustion)** of Jupiter or Venus, a period of several weeks to a couple of months when the planet is too close to the Sun to be visible, when weddings are traditionally deferred. Exact date ranges come from the almanac.

## By activity (traditional preferences)

The following are commonly cited summaries. They differ between schools, so treat them as indicative.

**Travel (yatra).** Favour Chara (movable) and Kshipra (swift) nakshatras such as Punarvasu, Swati, Shravana, Dhanishta, Ashwini, Pushya, Hasta, and the Mridu group; weekdays of Moon, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus are usually liked. Many almanacs also list **Disha Shool** ("direction spike"): East on Monday and Saturday, North on Tuesday and Wednesday, South on Thursday, West on Sunday and Friday are the directions traditionally avoided for starting a journey on those days. Avoid Chandrashtama and Rahu Kaal. Many people simply carry on with essential travel regardless.

**New venture or business (vyapara arambha).** Prefer a Shukla-paksha period with a Moon that is waxing, a Nanda/Bhadra/Jaya/Purna tithi (avoid Rikta), Wednesday or Thursday or Friday depending on the business, Kshipra or Dhruva nakshatras, and a lagna in which the lord is strong. Abhijit can serve when other options are limited.

**Marriage (vivaha).** The most elaborate muhurta. Common elements: months and seasons per regional custom; Jupiter and Venus not combust (asta) and not in childhood or old age phases in some schools; avoiding Chaturmas, Adhika masa, Kharmas; nakshatras traditionally favoured for weddings are Rohini, Mrigashira, Magha, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha, Mula, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada and Revati (lists vary by school); avoid Rikta tithis and Amavasya, avoid Vishti karana; a lagna with no malefic in the 7th or 8th and a strong Moon and Venus; plus compatibility checks (Ashtakoota/Guna Milan, see the compatibility files). Local customs are very significant, so refer to the family priest's calendar.

**Griha pravesh (housewarming).** Prefer Dhruva and soft nakshatras (Rohini, Uttara Phalguni, Uttara Ashadha, Uttara Bhadrapada, Mrigashira, Chitra, Anuradha, Revati), a fixed (sthira) lagna, and a Shukla-paksha, non-Rikta tithi. Months are often given as Magha, Phalguna, Vaishakha and Jyeshtha in several sources, but regional lists differ; avoid Chaturmas, Adhika masa and Panchaka periods.

**Namakarana (naming ceremony).** Traditionally on the 11th or 12th day after birth (some families use the 10th, or the 13th, or a later auspicious day), with a supportive nakshatra and avoiding Rikta tithis. Many families also pick the first syllable from the baby's birth nakshatra pada; see `nakshatras_deep_dive.md`.

**Vehicle or major purchase.** Prefer Kshipra, Chara or Mridu nakshatras (Ashwini, Pushya, Hasta, Punarvasu, Revati, Anuradha are typical choices), Thursday or Friday, a Shukla tithi that is not Rikta, and avoiding Chandrashtama; Tuesday is avoided by some as a sharp day. Treat any such timing as a traditional preference only, never as financial advice.

**Starting education (vidyarambha) and learning.** Wednesday and Thursday, Pushya, Hasta, Mrigashira, Revati, Anuradha, Punarvasu are commonly liked; Vasant Panchami (Magha Shukla Panchami) is a popular day.

## How to present muhurta to users

Offer two or three "traditionally preferred" windows, mention one or two cautions in plain terms, and say that the almanac of the user's own tradition takes precedence. Avoid fear language; use "tradition suggests picking another slot for beginnings if possible". Do not present muhurta as a guarantee for marriage, business or health outcomes.
